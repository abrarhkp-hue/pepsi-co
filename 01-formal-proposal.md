# Formal Proposal: Transborder Shipment Movement into Saudi Arabia
## Sohar Port (Oman) | Khorfakkan Port (UAE) | Fujairah Port (UAE)

**Prepared for:** PepsiCo — Supply Chain & Logistics Division  
**Prepared by:** Logistics Planning Team  
**Date:** March 2026  
**Document Status:** Draft for Review

---

## 1. Executive Summary

PepsiCo operates one of the largest fast-moving consumer goods (FMCG) supply chains in the Middle East. To meet growing demand in the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (KSA) and to build supply-chain resilience, this proposal outlines a structured approach for transborder shipment movements originating from or transiting through three strategic ports: **Sohar Port (Oman)**, **Khorfakkan Port (UAE)**, and **Fujairah Port (UAE)**.

The proposal covers recommended routing, customs compliance, estimated transit timelines, cost structures, and risk mitigation strategies. The primary objective is to provide PepsiCo with a reliable, cost-efficient, and compliant framework for managing cross-border goods movement into Saudi Arabia, ensuring uninterrupted supply to warehouses and distribution centres across the Kingdom.

> **Key Recommendation:** A multi-port strategy leveraging all three ports — with Sohar as the primary eastern gateway and Khorfakkan/Fujairah as redundancy and overflow routes — is recommended to optimise cost, transit time, and supply continuity.

## 5. Customs and Compliance Requirements

### 5.1 Saudi Arabia — Import Requirements

All goods entering the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia must comply with:

- **Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (SASO)** certification requirements for applicable product categories (food, beverages, packaging)
- **Saudi Customs Authority (Zakat, Tax and Customs Authority — ZATCA)** declarations and e-manifest submissions through the **Fasah** single-window platform
- **Halal certification** for all food and beverage products (issued by SASO-accredited bodies)
- **Product registration** with the Saudi Food and Drug Authority (SFDA) for food products
- Valid **Certificate of Origin** (CoO) — GCC CoO preferred for GCC-manufactured goods; non-GCC goods require standard CoO
- **Commercial Invoice**, **Packing List**, **Bill of Lading / Airway Bill**
- Import duty rates: typically 5% for most FMCG goods (0% for GCC-origin goods under the GCC Common Market rules)

### 5.2 Oman — Export / Transit Requirements (Sohar)

- Submission of export declaration through **Bayan** customs system (Oman Customs)
- For transit goods: **Carnet de Passage** or **Transit Bond** required at Oman customs
- Certificate of Origin from Oman Chamber of Commerce and Industry (OCCI)
- Bilateral trade agreements: Oman–KSA under the **Greater Arab Free Trade Area (GAFTA)** and GCC Customs Union

### 5.3 UAE — Export / Transit Requirements (Khorfakkan & Fujairah)

- Submission through **Dubai Trade** or **Sharjah Customs** / **Fujairah Customs** portal
- **Exit declaration** (GCC unified customs declaration) required for all goods exiting UAE to KSA
- Free Zone re-export: if goods are stored in a UAE Free Zone (Sharjah FZ, Fujairah FZ), additional re-export documentation is required
- **E-mirsal** system for Sharjah Customs; **Fujairah Customs** portal for Fujairah
- Transit guarantee / bond for goods in transit through UAE

## 7. Risk Assessment and Mitigation

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|:----------:|:------:|------------|
| Border crossing delays (KSA customs) | Medium | High | Pre-clearance via Fasah; use approved customs broker; ensure complete documentation |
| Port congestion at Khorfakkan | Low–Medium | Medium | Monitor port bulletins; activate Fujairah or Sohar as alternative |
| Documentation rejection at ZATCA | Medium | High | Pre-validate with customs broker; use document checklist; engage SFDA pre-approval |
| Road freight capacity shortfall | Low | High | Maintain frame contracts with 2+ road carriers per route |
| Regulatory changes (import duties, SASO requirements) | Low | Medium | Subscribe to ZATCA/SASO regulatory updates; quarterly compliance review |
| Product quality deterioration in transit | Low | High | Use reefer containers for temperature-sensitive products; specify transit time SLAs |
| Currency / payment risks | Low | Low | Use USD-denominated contracts; include FX escalation clause |
| Political / border closure risk | Very Low | Very High | Maintain minimum 4-week safety stock in KSA warehouses |

## 8. Recommendation for PepsiCo

Based on the analysis above, the following strategy is recommended:

1. **Designate Sohar Port as the primary gateway** for shipments destined for KSA's eastern region (Dammam, Al Khobar, Jubail). Sohar's proximity to the KSA border, competitive port charges, and strong road links make it the most cost-efficient option for eastern KSA.

2. **Utilise Khorfakkan Port for shipments from high-frequency vessel services** (Asia–Europe corridors), particularly for central and western KSA destinations. Khorfakkan's high vessel call frequency reduces vessel waiting time.

3. **Retain Fujairah Port as a flex/overflow route**, especially during peak season or when Jebel Ali and Khorfakkan face congestion. Fujairah's free-zone advantages can also be leveraged for bonded storage.

4. **Establish master agreements** with at least two licensed customs brokers in Saudi Arabia and one in each of Oman and UAE to ensure seamless border processing.

5. **Integrate shipment tracking** with PepsiCo's TMS (Transport Management System) to provide real-time visibility across all three port corridors.

6. **Maintain safety stock** of 3–4 weeks in KSA distribution centres to buffer against border delays or shipping disruptions.
